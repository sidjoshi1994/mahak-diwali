What it is

One night, a billion lamps

Diwali falls on the new-moon night of the Hindu month of Kartik, in October or November. It is the darkest night of the month, and everyone answers it with light.

How it unfolds

Five days, each with its own job

Think of it as a festive week. Tap each day.

Weeks before

First, the whole house gets scrubbed

Every home gets a deep clean, a fresh coat of paint and new curtains. The belief is simple: Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity, only visits clean, bright homes.

Then come the decorations: a toran of marigolds and mango leaves over the door, fairy lights on the balcony, and a rangoli on the doorstep.

Scrub the dusty door to clean it

On the doorstep

Rangoli: art made of coloured powder

Families draw these on the floor by hand, pinching coloured powder, rice flour or flower petals between their fingers. They last a few days and get swept away.

Draw anywhere in the circle. Your strokes are mirrored like a real rangoli.

The heart of it

Every edge of the house gets a flame

A diya is a small clay lamp filled with oil or ghee and a cotton wick. On Diwali night they line doorsteps, windowsills, balconies and rooftops.

The main night

Lakshmi Puja: the family prayer

At sunset the whole family dresses up and gathers at home for one prayer together. It is less like a church service and more like a family dinner with a ritual at the start.

  1. 1An altar with flowers, coins, sweets and a lamp, for Lakshmi (prosperity) and Ganesha (new beginnings).
  2. 2Aarti: someone circles a plate holding a flame while everyone sings. You wave your hands over the flame and touch your eyes for blessing.
  3. 3Then the doors open, the lamps go out to every corner and the sweets get passed around.

The real reason everyone loves Diwali

Mithai: boxes and boxes of sweets

Everyone gifts sweets to everyone: neighbours, colleagues, the building guard. By the end of the week a typical home has a stack of boxes on the dining table.

Open the box. Tap each sweet to see what it is.

What the week looks like

New clothes, full houses

New outfits

Everyone wears something new: kurtas, sarees, lehengas. Bright colours, lots of gold.

Gifts

Dry fruits, sweets, new kitchenware, silver coins. Kids get money in an envelope.

House hopping

You visit relatives and neighbours all week, and every house feeds you.

Card nights

Families play cards late into the night. Small bets, loud arguments, great fun.
